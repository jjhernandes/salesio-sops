How We Run Meetings

Bad meetings aren't a calendar problem. They're boring because nobody fights about anything, and useless because we throw everything into one meeting. The fix: four types of meetings, each with one job.
Based on Death by Meeting, Patrick Lencioni
Whole team
October 2026
Why

The two problems with meetings

01
No drama
A movie hooks you in the first 10 minutes with conflict. Most meetings do the opposite. Everyone stays polite, avoids the hard topic, and checks out.
Fix: open every meeting with what's at stake. “We're at 40% of the monthly cash goal with 9 days left. Here's why that matters.” Then go after the real disagreements, not around them.
02
No structure
Lencioni calls it “meeting stew.” Admin, numbers, strategy and people issues all in one call. Nobody knows what kind of meeting it is, so nothing gets fully solved.
Fix: separate meetings by purpose. Quick stuff stays quick. Big decisions get real time.
Bad meetings cost more than time. They lead to bad decisions, and bad decisions cost revenue.
The System

Four meetings, four jobs

Daily Check-In
5-10 min. What's on today. Who's blocked. No problem solving.
Weekly Tactical
45-90 min. Numbers, priorities, fix this week's problems.
Monthly Strategic
2-4 hrs. 1-2 big topics. Real debate. Real decisions.
Quarterly Off-Site
1-2 days. Step back. Strategy, team, people, market.
Meeting 1

Daily Check-In (5-10 min)

Who: sales team (setters + closers) and whoever is on the floor that day.
When: same time every day, start of shift. Slack huddle or quick Zoom. Stand up if you're in person.
Each person answers:
1. What's on my plate today (calls booked, follow-ups, key tasks)
2. Anything blocking me
Rules: no problem solving. If something needs more than 30 seconds, take it offline right after. Never skip it because you're busy. Once it gets skipped twice, it dies.
Meeting 2

Weekly Tactical (45-90 min)

01
Lightning round (~10 min)
Everyone gets 60 seconds: your top 2-3 priorities this week. No discussion. This shows the whole team where everyone's energy is going.
02
Scoreboard (~5 min)
Run the same numbers every week. Green, yellow or red vs. goal:
Cash collected · Calls booked · Show rate · Close rate · New students · Renewals + upsells · Content posted
Then check the #project-management board: anything red gets an owner and a new date.
03
Real-time agenda (the rest)
Don't set the agenda ahead of time. Build it from what the lightning round and the scoreboard just showed. Talk about whatever threatens this month's numbers first.
No strategy in the tactical. If a big topic comes up (pricing, hiring, a new channel), park it. It goes to the monthly strategic, or an ad-hoc strategic if it can't wait.
04
Close it out (~5 min)
1. What did we decide?
2. Who owns each action, by when? Goes into Notion Master Tasks today.
3. What are we telling the team or students, and who's telling them? If we don't say it out loud, everyone leaves with a different version.
Meeting 3

Monthly Strategic (2-4 hrs)

Who: leadership.
Topics: 1-2 max. Example topics: offer pricing or renewal structure, hiring a closer, a YouTube strategy shift, team comp plan.
Prep: whoever owns the topic sends a one-page brief 48 hours before. The problem, the data, the options. Read it before you show up.
Run it: present, debate, decide. JJ makes the final call if there's no agreement.
!
Mine for conflict
The leader's job is to pull disagreement out, not smooth it over. If someone's quiet, ask them directly: “What would make this fail?”
When it gets heated, say it out loud: “This is good. This is exactly the conversation we need.” That gives people permission to keep going.
Disagree and commit. Once a decision is made, everyone backs it outside the room. Log it in Notion Ideas & Decisions.
+
Ad-hoc strategic
When a strategic issue can't wait for the monthly, book a separate 1-2 hour session just for that topic. Don't cram it into the weekly.
Meeting 4

Quarterly Off-Site (1-2 days)

Where: out of the normal routine. In person if possible. Idea: run it the day before or after a quarterly mastermind in Orlando while everyone's already in town.
Cover four things:
1. Strategy: are we on track for $350K/month? What changed? What do we stop doing?
2. Team: how are we working together? Where are we slow? Where is communication breaking?
3. People: who's crushing it, who needs support. Leadership only. This part stays private and never goes in public notes or recaps.
4. Market: what are other sales trainers doing? What are students asking for that we don't have?
Every Meeting

House rules

1
Know what type of meeting it is before it starts
2
Open with what's at stake
3
One owner runs it and keeps time
4
Fathom records it. The recap and tasks get built automatically
5
Speak up in the room, not in the DMs after
6
Every action has an owner + due date in Master Tasks
7
End with: what are we telling people, and who's telling them
8
Start on time. End on time
