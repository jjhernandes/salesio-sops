Funnel and Nurture

  1. DM Ad → comment → DM setter conversation → booked call. This is the whole funnel now.No quiz funnel, no separate "straight to book" path, no phone setters
  2. Fast cash emails. JJ has a proven sequence (accountable for $6.4M in revenue across his own past teams) he's sending over: broken-link resend, a 9-word "do you want this" email, a deposit-based win-back offer
  3. Monthly warm webinar stays as a secondary nurture play, not the primary funnel
  4. Win-back call for no-shows/non-closers. A live group call (Khalil/Jazz host) for anyone who booked but didn't close, offering a special deal or deposit option. No extra ad spend needed, these are already-warm leads

Owner for responses/replies on the DM/email nurture side wasn't fully settled on the call, worth a direct follow-up.

3

Automations

  • GHL as the backend for email/nurture
  • Slack connection for new leads. New submission pings the team immediately, same pattern JJ ran at scale elsewhere: a lead comes in, gets tagged, and everyone can see it's been picked up
  • Sales call recordings auto-post to Slack. Zoom recording → unlisted YouTube upload → posted in a public channel automatically via Zapier, so any call can be reviewed by anyone
  • BrightBoard connected to GHL. End-of-day submission forms populate the dashboard automatically, nobody has to manually build a report
  • Setter feedback loop on ad source. Hyros can't track a click once it's inside a DM conversation, so setters have to self-report which ad a lead came from. This is how they identified their best-performing ad last time and shut off the rest
4

Tracking

  • Wistia for video engagement
  • BrightBoard as the central dashboard: closer numbers, setter numbers, upcoming calls, full form history, all filterable by individual team member
  • Hyros for ad-level tracking (limited once leads move into DMs, see automations above)
  • Self-reporting via daily end-of-day forms for both closers and setters, feeding BrightBoard directly

Weekly rhythm: a Friday call where every setter and closer pulls up their own BrightBoard numbers and reports live to the team. This is the accountability mechanism JJ says fixed the same problem on past teams, not a manager chasing people down mid-week.

5

Sales

Package length: real debate on the call between 3, 6, and 12 months. The 12-month is the better deal in principle (higher LTV, matches how JJ would price this "every single time" if cash flow weren't a factor), but given the current cash crunch, the call landed on starting with a lower barrier of entry now to rebuild volume and cash flow, then graduating back up once stable. Key caveat stated directly: you can offer a cheap entry tier, but you can't easily walk it back up once it's out there, so this is meant to be a circumstantial move, not a permanent repositioning.

  • No financing on the entry-tier offer, this protects margin and keeps the door open to a backend extension/upsell later
  • Coaches get 10% on any renewal, same as a closer, so retention-quality coaching is directly incentivized

The math, worked live on the call: at a $7,000 blended AOV and a worst-case 25% close rate, hitting the $250K target needs roughly 144 calls to show, which means around 206 booked calls, call it 10 bookings a day across both accounts. Historically the team has hit 230+ booked calls/month at a 25–30% close rate and a $6,200 blended AOV, so this is in range of what's already been proven, not a new stretch.

The Respark role is new and deliberate: its only job is reviving cold conversations and keeping the pipeline organized on a spreadsheet, so Micah and the other setters spend their time on live, high-intent conversations instead of chasing stale leads. This is also the direct fix for the "whose lead is this" conflict that caused real team friction in the past.

Trial process for new setters: roleplay conversation with Jalen and Micah before going live, then a trial period with daily KPI accountability, missing a day's KPI is an automatic cut, formal review at day 7 to decide who stays.
