The Offer

Two phases, one committed deal

This isn't a cheaper package. It's a trade: give up $1,000 on month one in exchange for killing the 7-day refund window entirely. Every $3,750 sale today carries 7 days of refund risk. This removes that risk and locks 60 days of revenue instead.

PhasePriceDeliverableTrial

Total committed value$6,500Sold and contracted as one 60-day term
⚠️
Decide This Before Launch

Is month 2 locked at the point of sale, or does the client decide after month 1 whether to continue? If it's optional, the exact drop-off problem this was built to solve just moves 30 days downstream, and closers will default to selling only the easy $2,750 month. Recommendation: lock it. Sell and contract this as one 60-day, two-phase agreement collected as two payments, not two separate decisions.

Execution Plan

Rollout, in order

  1. Lock the month-2 question above. Nothing below should go into scripts or paperwork until this is confirmed.Owner: Eddie
  2. Update the contract. No 7-day trial clause on this offer, 60-day minimum term covering both phases, and clear language on what happens if a client tries to cancel mid-term.
  3. Build the CRM pipeline. A separate stage for "Branding Roadmap" deals with the 60-day term and the phase-2 transition date tracked, so nothing relies on someone remembering to follow up.
  4. Reposition the pitch. This goes into the offer stack as Phase 1 for any client, not as a fallback when someone says no to $3,750. Every client needs branding whether they run ads or not, so it's presented that way from the top of the call, not introduced after an objection. Selling it as a discount because someone couldn't afford the full package reads as needy and costs the frame.
  5. Brief the team as a standard, not an option. Once contract and CRM are ready, this is "the new entry point," not an optional add-on reps can ignore.
  6. Road-test on call reviews before it's fully live, the same way any new framework gets pressure-tested before it's trusted on live calls.

Compare that to $300–450 for selling the $3,750 package alone with no phase 1. That gap is intentional: it makes the full roadmap worth meaningfully more to the closer than either piece sold on its own, which is what gets the team to sell it instead of defaulting to whichever feels like the easier yes in the moment.
