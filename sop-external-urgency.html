External Urgency: For the “Will I Actually Use It?” Buyer

The buyer wants it but doesn't trust themselves to follow through. This isn't a price objection. It's a belief objection. Break the belief, then give them a real reason to act now.
Built from Mo x JJ, Oct 19
Belief objection
Low ticket + high ticket
Spot It

What it sounds like

“Will I actually use it?”
“I have a really bad pattern of...” / “This is a recurring pattern.”
“It ultimately comes down to me doing the work.”
“I'm scared to post.” / “I want to take it easy at first.”
“I want to say yes so bad, but I can't, because I know myself.”
Why it happens: a lot of these buyers have focus or attention issues. Unless something is actually urgent, they won't move.
Step 1

Don't make it worse

Don't say: “If you don't mess with it after a week, just WhatsApp me for a refund.” You're planting the exact fear they have: that they won't use it. Don't bring up the refund unless they ask.
Don't say: “What's the worst case scenario?” You don't want them picturing the worst case. Say: “What's coming to mind?”
Don't say: “There's no pressure” if you told them it's a one-time offer. There is pressure, they feel it, and now they don't trust you. Own it: “There's a decision here today, and I want it to be the right one for you.”
Don't lead with a 30-day guarantee. Too long for a low-ticket offer. Clawbacks go up and it attracts the try-it-and-refund buyer. Take it off the slide and keep a 7-day version in reserve.
Step 2

Open it up

“What's coming to mind?”
Then let them talk. Listen for the belief underneath: “I never follow through,” “I get excited and quit.”
Step 3

Break the belief: light first, then direct

01
The light open
“What, you got ADHD or something?”
Said as a joke. Whatever they answer (“I do,” “undiagnosed but definitely something,” “nope”), it goes to the same place.
02
The turn
“Okay. So are you going to keep letting that be the reason you don't do what you want to do? Or is this the time to make the change?”
03
The choice
“Whether you buy this or not, being intentional is a choice. Would you agree?”
04
The yeses
“Do you see yourself using the tool?” Yes.
“Do you feel like it can be an asset to you?” Yes.
“So are you going to decide with me to make the shift? Or are we going to keep going on the same path?”
05
If they still hold back, get direct
“You're aware you get distracted. That's fine. But right now you're choosing to be your own roadblock. Is anyone physically stopping you? No. It's just you. This has nothing to do with the tool or whether it works for you. It's a decision you need to make for yourself. So are you ready to make it?”
Why it works: their belief is “I'm attention deficient, so I won't follow through.” The joke gets them to admit it. The questions show them it's a choice, not a fact.
Only works if you're the authority. If you haven't earned that by this point in the call, skip the direct version.
Step 4

Create external urgency

For people who only move when something is urgent, give them a real opportunity cost that runs out. Think to-do list or a timer: something outside them that makes them move.
Rule: the urgency has to be real. Never invent a deadline. If you say it's a one-time offer, it is.
The offer: “This plan is only available on this call. After today it's back to the regular renewal.”
The cost of waiting: tie it to the pain they told you. “You told me you're tired of zero clients one month and 10 the next. Every month you wait to start posting is another month of that.”
Their own pattern: “You just told me you always wait until you feel ready. Waiting is the pattern. Starting today is how you break it.”
Momentum on the call: “Let's set it up together right now so you leave with your first post done.” They leave having already used it, which kills “will I use it?”
Step 5

The guarantee, only if needed

Keep it off the main slide. Only bring it up if they're still skeptical after Steps 2 to 4. Use the 7-day version as a safety net, not the reason to buy:
“And if something's off in the first 7 days, we'll make it right. But I don't think that's going to be the issue here.”
