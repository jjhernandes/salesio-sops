JJ Hernandez — Closing System

Everything you learned running Iman Gadzhi's sales team, restructured into five ownable systems. Intro and outro are fully scripted — read them straight to camera. Each system follows the same four-beat shape.

Scripted — Read As Written
Intro

High ticket closing is hard. It's not as easy as everyone makes it seem. Until you REALLY understand why you're actually losing deals.

You're on a call, everything feels right, and then something shifts slightly during the call… and by the time you notice it, the deal is already gone. I was that closer for years.

Then I stopped fixing the "wrong thing" and started building the right system to overcome it.

Twenty million dollars closed. Sold for Hormozi, for Iman Gadzhi, for the biggest people you're learning from right now. And the system behind those numbers is what nobody in this space is actually teaching.

Today I'm giving you five parts of that system. And at the end, one question that tells you instantly whether you're operating like a top closer or just thinking you are one.

Let's get into it.

Production Reference — Not Spoken On Camera
  1. Name it — give the system an ownable name, not a description.
  2. Test it — a quick question that makes the viewer check themselves before you explain anything.
  3. Explain and prove it — what most closers get wrong, what the correct version looks like, one real story.
  4. Close and transition — land the takeaway, then create curiosity about the next system.
System One — The Foundation
Beat 1 — Name It

"First up, system one. I call this The Belief Transfer."

Beat 2 — Test It

"Quick gut check before I explain this. Think about your last call. Did you actually believe, underneath everything, that what you were selling was going to change their life? Or were you just hoping they'd say yes?"

Beat 3 — Explain & Prove It

"Because here's what nobody tells you. Sales is a transfer of belief. That's it. That's the whole game. And you cannot transfer a belief you don't actually have. I don't care how good your script is. It might be your hundredth call of the week. But it's their first time hearing this. Their whole life might change on this call. If you treat it like number one hundred, they feel it. I've watched reps who sound confident on the surface, and the buyer still says no — because underneath, they didn't believe it either. That's not a flaw you hide. That's the actual thing you have to go fix."

Beat 4 — Close & Transition

"Fix how you feel about what you're selling, and everything else you do on the call gets easier. But belief only gets you to the phone. What happens in the minutes before you actually dial? That's system two."

System Two — Before The Call
Beat 1 — Name It

"System two. I call this Setting The Intention."

Beat 2 — Test It

"Before your next call — could you say out loud, right now, why you actually want this person to say yes? Is it for them? Or is it for your commission check?"

Beat 3 — Explain & Prove It

"Here's why this matters more than almost anything else you do. I can say the exact same two words to you, two completely different ways. 'Screw you, JJ.' One's an insult. One's between friends, laughing. Same words. Opposite meaning. The words carried nothing. The intention behind them carried everything. That's what's happening on every single call you take. Your intention leaks through before you've said a word that matters. Decide it before you dial — not during the call, when you're already scrambling."

Beat 4 — Close & Transition

"Get your intention right before you pick up the phone, and the buyer feels it before you even get to your first question. But once you're on the call, most reps get the very next part backwards completely."

System Three — The Reframe
Beat 1 — Name It

"System three. I call this The Feeling Framework."

Beat 2 — Test It

"Think about the last time a prospect told you they needed to 'think about it.' Be honest — was that actually about logic? Or was it about how the call felt?"

Beat 3 — Explain & Prove It

"Because here's the belief that's costing you the most deals right now. People do not buy with logic. Neuroscience proves it — humans buy on how they feel at the end of the call, then they justify it with logic afterward. So think about what happens when you lead with logic. You dump features. You handle every objection perfectly. You sound smart. And they still say 'let me think about it.' That's not a logic problem. They didn't feel it. I was coaching a rep of mine, Lisa, on exactly this. I told her: think about a bed for your kid. If your kid's sleeping on the floor and you find the right bed, right price, right fit — you don't ask your six-year-old for permission. You just get it. Because you feel it's right. That's what we're doing on a call. We're not informing people. We're making the problem felt. At Gym Launch, one question every rep asked was 'what's your runway?' — and you'd watch it hit them that they're three months from zero. That discomfort is what moves people. You cannot logic someone into that feeling."

Beat 4 — Close & Transition

"Your job isn't to make the buyer comfortable. Your job is to be comfortable making them uncomfortable. But feeling it isn't enough on its own — because I've watched reps nail this part perfectly and still lose the deal. System four is why."

System Four — The Tactic
Beat 1 — Name It

"System four. I call this The Silence Weapon."

Beat 2 — Test It

"Think about your last call. Be honest with yourself — how many times were you just waiting for your turn to talk?"

Beat 3 — Explain & Prove It

"That's the leak. Right there. Here's the single most tactical thing in this entire video, and it's the opposite of everything you've been taught. It's not saying more. It's saying nothing. You give a statement, not another question, and then you shut up. Watch how it works. A prospect tells you they're closing at ten percent. Instead of firing your next question, you just go: '10%… hmm.' Nine times out of ten, they keep talking. 'Yeah, I've been trying to hit twenty but I'm stuck.' There it is. The real answer. The thing they'd never have told you if you'd just asked your next question. Nobody's comfortable in silence. That's exactly why it's a superpower. Most reps physically cannot hold it for two seconds. The one rep who can pulls out information nobody else gets."

Beat 4 — Close & Transition

"That single habit will tell you more about a deal than your next ten questions combined. But even with perfect feeling and perfect silence, I've watched reps still sabotage the close in the final stretch. That's system five."

System Five — The Operating Level
Beat 1 — Name It

"System five. And this is the one that separates a closer who's grinding from one who's actually operating. I call this Same Side Of The Table."

Beat 2 — Test It

"Have you ever felt a deal slipping mid-call, and you either pushed harder, or you quietly backed off? Be honest. Both of those kill the deal."

Beat 3 — Explain & Prove It

"Here's what almost every rep gets backwards. The second a deal feels like it's slipping, they do one of two things. They hard-push, or they pull back — 'ah, you know, maybe this isn't for you.' Both kill it. The move is to sit on the same side of the table. You're not across from them selling at them. You're next to them, solving with them. I had a guy on a call who thought he had to cancel, because someone else was already building his funnel and he figured he had to choose. One reframe: 'why not do both?' That's it. He booked 4:30 that same day. He wasn't objecting. He just needed someone beside him to see it clearly. And here's the thing almost nobody in this space actually teaches — the deal isn't won in the pitch. It's won in discovery, before the pitch even starts. No pitch saves a weak discovery. You can have the smoothest close in the world, and it's dead on arrival if you didn't do the work up front."

Beat 4 — Close & Transition

"Detaching is what closes deals. Chasing is what loses them. That's the difference between a closer who's grinding every month hoping something changes, and one who's actually operating a system. And that brings me to the one question that tells you exactly which one you are."

The Final Exam

"Here's the one question I ask every closer who sits down across from me, when I'm trying to figure out where they actually are. It's the one that makes the honest ones uncomfortable."

"When's the last time you got off a call and felt like you actually helped someone — not like you closed them?"

[ pause — let it sit ]

"If you had to think about it… that's not a script problem. That's not a tonality problem. That's a belief problem. And no system on this list fixes that until you do."
