Framework

What makes this sequence work

01
Proof before ask
Screenshots and testimonials go up top, before the pitch. Results carry the argument, not adjectives.
02
Frame it as their benefit
"We're raising the price" reads as greed. "We're protecting quality/service for a smaller group" reads as care.
03
Real urgency, stated plainly
"No fake scarcity. No BS tactics." A real clock, a real date, said once, not manufactured hype.
04
Preempt the excuses
Name the objections before they show up ("I was stuck in traffic," "my boss locked me in my office") so late replies have nowhere to land.
05
Stack the value in dollars
Every deliverable gets a price tag next to it, so the new price looks small next to the total stack.
06
Borrow authority
Name-drop the coaches and the names behind them (who they've built sales teams for) to raise perceived stakes.

Adapting this

For a $1K raise, not a 4x one

Tone it down. "Doubling in price," "gone, no exceptions," "I'll probably never run a promotion again" — that language matches a 4x jump. For +$1K, one clear notice plus a short countdown does the job. Overselling the urgency on a small raise reads as try-hard.
Lead with the CS system, not the coaches. Template 4's authority stack works because it's citing outside names. Your value-add is the new proactive CS/concierge system Mino is building — use that as the "here's what justifies it" the way Justin already framed it for Alan.
Loop the sales team in first. JJ's note from the call: whatever you send, give Omar, Allan, Eli, and Justin a heads-up before it goes out, so they're not caught flat-footed by a colder or more skeptical lead on a call.
One email, maybe two. You don't need all 4 templates for a $1K move. Template 1 (announce) plus a shortened Template 2 (warn, with the CS system swapped in for the bonus list) likely covers it.
