The Non-Negotiables
  1. Max 3 touches per day. Call, voicemail, and text each count as one touch — that's TCPA law, not a suggestion (California fines run ~$1,000 per violation). Three touches in a day is the ceiling, not the target.
  2. Never double-dial. Calling back-to-back reads as "trying to sell me immediately" — same energy as a door-knocker skipping rapport. Only exception: you got hung up on instantly or hit dead air, and it's genuinely a dropped-connection callback.
  3. Respond the second a lead engages — mid-dinner, doesn't matter. That's the only window where you have their attention. Every other rule about pacing is about touches you initiate, not about them coming back to you.

Speed & Timing

Call new leads within 1–5 minutes of them coming in. Beyond that, three windows in the day actually convert.

8–10 AM
Guys in the trades are heading into the field early. Catch them before the day swallows them.
1–3 PM
Never 12–1 — that's lunch and they won't pick up. By early afternoon they're tired, lackadaisical, and actually looking for a reason to break focus. That's your opening.
5–7 PM
Out of the field, settling down for the day. Calling at 6:36 PM and hearing "yeah, I was just getting ready for bed" is normal — it still lands.

Not Booked — The 7-Touch Sequence

If they don't answer or don't book on the first call, this is the order. Each step gives them room before the next one lands.

1
Text immediately if no answer
Low pressure, states why you're calling. Don't over-format it — two casual sentences, not a template with line breaks and a sign-off.
Script"Hey Rick, this is Chris with Social Era Media — when's a good time to speak?"
Script (JJ's line)"Hey, I spoke with the team, have something they want to share with you. When's a good time to speak for 10 minutes?"
2
Wait until the next day
Give the first touch time to soak. This is the touch-limit rule in practice — you already used your day-1 touch.
3
Call again, leave a voicemail
Pattern break, not a repeat of touch 1. Go one layer deeper on why the call matters.
Script"Yeah, this is Chris with Social Era Media, still trying to talk to you — just need 10 minutes, trying to book a time to speak deeper and get a quick understanding of your business."
4
Send proof, not pressure
Pull a case study or portfolio piece matched to their actual service — a landscaper gets a landscaper's results, a plumber gets a plumber's. This is a give, not another ask.
Script"Hey, I was checking out your marketing for your company. Wanted you to check out a couple things we did for other companies — here's Royal Vistas Landscaping, here's Rosewood Landscaping."
5
Day 6, still no engagement — pattern interrupt
Send something genuinely funny or human — a relevant meme, not another pitch. It breaks the "serious salesperson" frame they've built around you. Worked on a contractor who'd gone dark for days — laughed, called the office same hour.
6
Final call — touch 7
A touch is a touch, however it lands. If nothing after this, the lead goes cold — but not dead.
Cold lead, long tail: after the 7 touches are exhausted, don't drop it. Call again 1 week later, then 2 weeks after that, then double it again to a month out — roughly 2 months of total follow-up. Unrecognized numbers get picked up more than you'd think, and you're back in the cycle.

Booked — The Triage Call

Goal of this call is not to sell. It's to get a read on who they are as a person and gather what you'll actually use to close later. If they push for a pitch, answer informatively — don't sway or convince.

1
Open by confirming the moment
Respect their time before you take any of it.
Script"Hey, this is Chris with Social Era Media — did I catch you at a good time?"
2
Revenue question
Frame it as making the call more focused, not as qualifying them. Get a specific number, not a range, then let them justify it — that justification is where the real story comes out.
Script"I'm just trying to get to know your business before we talk, so we can make the call more focused. You said revenue's under $50K — where are you at exactly? Tell me a bit more about the business."
3
Motivation question
This is where pain, goals, and proof all surface at once. Don't dig — just let them open up, and collect what they hand you. It becomes ammunition for the real call.
Script"What's got you most interested in using social media for your company?"
4
Ideal-project / niche question
Most contractors assume you'll market everything they do. Correct that here, and claim authority doing it — the riches are in the niches. Get them to name their most profitable project type, then the actual revenue and margin on it, and explain why narrowing wins.
Script"So on average, that project makes you this much revenue — how much of that is margin? Got it. Reason I ask: we could point our arrow at a lot of different targets, but if we do that, we don't land any of them. We want to get narrow."
5
Give them the floor
Open it up before you book. If you only ever take information, the call feels extractive — even if they can't consciously name why. This is also where you pre-handle objections and hand them a sense of control.
Script"Any questions for me on my end?"
Script (when asked what you do)"We live in the show-me times — people don't care what you tell them, they care about proof. We're The Proof in the Pudding, and we target contractors specifically because a $60 lead can turn into a $50–60K kitchen or bathroom remodel."
6
Book inside 48 hours
Never book further out than that. If they're free today, book today.
Script"When in the next 48 hours can we speak? If you've got time today, let's just do that."
7
Confirm, then send the post-book package
Send the post-booking video — call it a "5-minute video," not just "a video." Naming the length removes the friction of not knowing what they're committing to. Then send a testimonial or case study matched directly to whatever they said they cared about or feared, in the same text.
Script"Thanks for speaking with me — this is Chris with Social Era Media. Looking forward to our conversation on [time]."
