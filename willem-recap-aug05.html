Framework

The Four Levels of Communication

You were stuck on impulse / chemical / behavioral / electric. Here's the breakdown, ordered by processing speed:
Impulse→ Chemical (Intention)→ Behavioral→ Electric (What You Say)
Impulse is the fastest, it's pure energy, how you feel about yourself, felt the instant you walk into a room, before anything is said. Chemical is the emotional environment, felt before it's processed, this is why someone can sense bad intentions from a person even when the words sound fine (the corrupt cop example). Behavioral is pattern recognition kicking in, your brain scanning for reassurance about whether this is a good or bad scenario. Electric is what you actually say, the slowest layer, the brain's conceptual, electrical processing (literally neuroelectric, the same mechanism behind things like brain-wave therapy).
Framework

Cognitive Dissonance: You're Not Burning Reality, You're Revealing It

Your read was close: whoever controls the story that resolves someone's identity-level pain controls that person. You raised a good pushback, won't someone just mold reality to protect their identity if you challenge it directly? The distinction: you're not creating a new reality to fight them on, you're holding up a mirror to their true identity using their own evidence. They can't burn down what's actually true. That's the tension and separation that puts you in control, not manufactured conflict.
"You're not even creating reality. You're showing them their true identity. That's why the highest level of sales is holding the mirror up to people."
Framework

Belief Dictates Identity, Identity Dictates Action

This is the mechanism underneath cognitive dissonance, and it's why someone can stay loyal to a politician through 50 scandals, or stay in a relationship that's clearly bad for them. The belief comes first, identity adheres to the belief, and actions defend the identity, all the way up until the belief finally breaks.
Belief→ Identity→ Action
On calls, this means you challenge beliefs, not identity and not action directly. Not "what would you need to do to become that person" (action) and not "you sound like the type of person who..." (identity), but "what are you using to decide this is the right fit, how do you know?" That's a belief-level question, and it's the one that actually moves people.
Framework

Belief, Identity, or Pain: Just Catch the Belief

You asked whether to focus on beliefs, identity, or pain. Simple answer: catch the belief. Pain alone doesn't sell, if someone's in pain but their beliefs aren't aligned with buying, they won't move. But if their beliefs are already aligned, you don't need to challenge anything, some prospects walk in already sold and pushing on a belief that's already settled just creates resistance. Don't overthink this into a bigger framework than it is. Catch the belief, that's the whole job.

"There's a belief there that you're probably not questioning that puts you into this identity, that you're stuck. You're in more control than you think."
