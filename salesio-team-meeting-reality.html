What Actually Happens in a Sales.io Team Meeting

Not the version on the org chart. The real week — five days, one shared number, and a room that either builds a standard or lets one slide.

Sales.io · Team Ops

There's no all-hands slide deck. No "quick sync" that runs forty minutes over. The Sales.io team — Cole on coaching and student experience, Jalen running setters, Faris owning the pipeline data, Lu on the phones closing — runs on the same five-day cadence JJ built running sales teams before Sales.io existed. It's blunt, it's short, and every day has exactly one job.

Monday — the number gets said out loud

Nobody leaves the room without a commitment

Monday isn't a pep talk. It's twenty to thirty minutes, and the only thing that happens is this: every rep states their BAM — Bare Ass Minimum, the floor they're putting their name on for the week — and their Reach, the number that gets them out of bed on a Saturday if they're behind. They write it. They say it out loud. It gets written down in front of the room.

That's the entire mechanism. Nobody's being motivated. A public commitment was just made, and now it's real.

What it sounds like
JJ "What are you putting your name on the line for this week?"
Room Silence. Nobody rushes to fill it.
Rep States a number. It gets written down.

Then a short Q&A — what's happening out there, what needs to change, what does the rep need from their manager this week. Then it's over. Monday's whole purpose is speed: start the week fast, don't let the meeting become the excuse not to work.

Tuesday & Thursday — call review, or role play

Reactive one day, proactive the next

These two days rotate the same slot for a reason. Call review is reactive — pull a real recording, listen as a group, and the rep who took the call self-assesses before anyone else gets to speak. Role play is proactive — a realistic scenario is run before the rep ever needs it live: an inbound DM, a phone set, an objection that came up three times last week.

Whichever one happens Tuesday, the other happens Thursday. If it doesn't connect to something the rep will actually face that week, it gets cut — theory doesn't survive in this room.

"If it doesn't connect to a real situation they'll face this week, cut it."

— JJ Hernandez

Wednesday — the gut check

Half the week is gone. The numbers get pulled.

Wednesday is where the room stops being theoretical. Every rep's numbers get pulled — sets booked, shows, qualified sets, disqualified sets — and the gap between where they are and their Monday BAM gets addressed directly, in the room, not later. Nobody coasts to Friday without knowing exactly where they stand.

This is also the day setters and closers sit in the same room, because Wednesday is when the cross-reference runs — the process that stops both sides from quietly blaming each other.

A real cross-reference, walked through
Closer Flags 3 sets from the week as unqualified.
Question Where did those 3 come from? All from the same setter.
Setter "They said they had money, they were interested, they confirmed the call."
Review The recording gets pulled. The setter went surface-level — never confirmed real intent.
Outcome Now there's evidence, not just two people's competing story.

Data plus the actual recording. Never data alone — a number without evidence just starts an argument. Run this every Wednesday for the first month and unqualified sets get rare, not because anyone got scared, but because the room now shares one real definition of "qualified."

Friday — the one day with no numbers

Wins and shout-outs. Everyone, no exceptions.

Friday drops all of it — no data review, no BAM check, no pressure. Everyone in the room, every role, and the only thing that happens is a personal win from the week and a genuine shout-out to someone who showed up the way the team wants to show up. JJ goes first, every time. Then it goes around the room.

It's not scripted and it doesn't get rushed. The value doesn't show up in week one — it shows up in week three, when the pattern of who gets shouted out and who doesn't starts telling you more than the Wednesday numbers do.

What to actually watch: a rep who stops getting shouted out by week three isn't a coincidence. That's the signal to have a private conversation — before it shows up as a missed BAM.

Underneath It All — managing people, not a scoreboard

The numbers never lie. They also never explain themselves.

Every one of these five days runs on data — BAMs, cross-referenced sets, shows, cash collected. But the actual job underneath the cadence is reading what's happening in a rep's life before it shows up in their numbers. Money stress shows up in how someone handles an objection. A rough week at home shows up as a quieter voice on Monday.

"Who you are off your calls is who you are on your calls." That's not just what we teach students — it's what a manager has to see in their own team before the numbers confirm it.

— JJ Hernandez

The structure isn't the point. The standard the structure builds is the point — a team that doesn't want to let each other down, not one that's afraid of missing a number. Run this consistently and by week four, the room runs itself.
