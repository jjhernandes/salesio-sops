Sales.io — Team Standard

Overcommunication

The default failure mode on any sales team isn't a bad script — it's a gap. A setter who doesn't flag a bad lead, a closer who doesn't report an objection, a number nobody logged until Friday. This doc sets the standard: what closers and setters owe each other, how fast, in which channel, and why the tracking behind it is worth real money, not busywork.

01

The Core Rule

When in doubt, communicate. The cost of over-sharing a status update is thirty seconds of someone's attention. The cost of under-sharing is a wasted call, a misquoted price, or a closer walking into a room blind.

Default To
  • Log the moment it happens, not at end of day
  • Flag a blocker the same hour you hit it
  • Say "I don't know, checking" instead of going quiet
  • Over-explain a handoff rather than under-explain it
Never
  • Sit on bad news hoping it resolves itself
  • Batch updates for a "big reveal" at the weekly meeting
  • Assume someone already knows because it seems obvious
  • Let a lead sit untouched without a note explaining why

Which Channel, For What

This has drifted across teams and docs — Sales.io uses Slack, FitMama uses Telegram, and lead-specific context belongs in the CRM, never buried in a chat thread. Pick the right one every time.

ChannelUse It For
Close CRM (Sales.io)Anything specific to one Sales.io lead — handoff context, objections, qualification status. If it's about a person, it goes on their record, not in chat.
GHL (FitMama)Same function as Close CRM, for FitMama leads specifically — post-call notes, pipeline stage, SQL/NQL status.
Slack (Sales.io team)Team-wide status, daily EOD posts, blockers, help requests.
Telegram (FitMama team)Same function as Slack, for the FitMama team specifically — Friday KPI recaps, daily standups.
Direct messageOne-on-one coaching feedback, anything that names a specific person's performance gap. Never call someone out in a group channel.
Rule of thumb: if the information helps only you remember something later, it's a personal note. If it helps the next person in the chain do their job better, it goes in the shared system — CRM for lead-specific, team channel for everything else.

Setter → Closer: What's Owed

The full script and qualifying framework live in the Master Setting SOP. This is the communication half — what has to reach the closer, and by when.

1
A structured note the moment the call is booked
Not after the shift ends. Name, source, situation, pain point in their words, qualification level, energy/tone. A closer starting a call in the dark loses 5-10 minutes of a 45-minute call rebuilding rapport that should already exist.
2
Qualification status, explicitly, not implied
SQL vs. NQL, or whatever the current system calls it. Don't make the closer guess from context clues in the notes — say the status directly.
3
Anything unusual, flagged before the call, not during
A partner who needs to be looped in, a reschedule history, a red flag that almost got the lead disqualified. The closer should never discover something the setter already knew mid-call.
A Bad Handoff Looks Like
  • "He seemed interested" with nothing behind it
  • A name in the CRM with zero notes
  • Qualification tag applied without checking if it's actually true
04

Closer → Setter: The Loop Back

Communication is not one-directional. A setter can't improve qualification if the outcome of their sets disappears into a closer's calendar and never comes back.

1
Every disqualified set gets flagged back to the setter who booked it
Not as a callout — as data. Why wasn't this person qualified? That's the only way a setter's qualification skill actually improves week over week.
2
Outcome logged same-day: show, no-show, close, or lost
A setter should never have to ask "did that call happen?" three days later. If they booked it, they're owed the outcome.
3
Objection patterns get shared back, not just logged privately
If five closers are all hearing the same objection this week, that's a setting-stage or offer-stage problem, not a closing problem — and it only surfaces if closers actually say it out loud.

Daily Reporting

One end-of-day report, every rep, every day. Posted in team channel — Slack for Sales.io, Telegram for FitMama.

RoleReports
SettersContacts reached, new leads in, closes, cash collected (week-to-date and month-to-date), what went well, what could've gone better, whether help is needed.
ClosersCalls on calendar, shows, offers made, no-shows, reschedules, closes, cash collected, week-to-date and month-to-date cash collected, same reflection questions.

Daily reports roll into a weekly rhythm. This is where BAM (bare minimum) and Reach (stretch) targets get set, checked, and enforced. Sales.io and FitMama run their own versions of this — same shape, different details — so don't assume one team's schedule is the other's.

DaySales.ioFitMama
MondayEvery rep states BAM and Reach for the week out loud. 20-30 minutes, public commitment.Team reviews last week's BAM & Reach vs. actuals, then sets this week's live.
Tue / ThuCall Review or Roleplay, alternating.—
WednesdayMidweek stat check + setter/closer cross-reference: closer flags an unqualified set, traces it back to who booked it, setter explains, manager pulls the recording if needed.—
FridayCulture Day — wins and shoutouts, all hands, no data reviewed.KPI report posted in Telegram by every rep: calls, applications, bookings, deals closed, biggest win, biggest blocker.

Why Tracking Isn't Optional

Every number a setter or closer logs feeds a decision someone else makes with real money behind it. Skipping the log doesn't make the work go away — it just means the next decision gets made blind.

1
It's the only way to find which lever is broken
Connect rate, booking rate, show rate, close rate — a problem in any one of these looks identical from the outside ("numbers are down") unless someone actually has the funnel logged stage by stage. Fix the wrong lever and nothing improves.
2
It's what separates a setter problem from a closer problem
The Wednesday cross-reference only works because sets are tracked back to the person who booked them. Without that, a bad week gets blamed on whoever's easiest to blame instead of whoever's actually responsible.
3
It's the difference between a $60K mistake and a caught one
FitMama's old funnel ran for a month burning spend with no return before anyone could prove it wasn't working — because the tracking to catch it early wasn't tight enough. Good tracking is what lets a bad bet get killed in week one instead of month three.
4
It rolls up into whether the company hits its number
Every BAM is a personal commitment that ladders into a team target, which ladders into the company's monthly revenue goal. A setter who doesn't log a set isn't just skipping paperwork — they're making the company's real-time picture of its own revenue less accurate.
The honest version: a number that isn't logged isn't just missing — it's actively misleading, because everyone downstream assumes the numbers they can see are the complete picture. Under-tracking is worse than no tracking, because it looks like data.

Non-Negotiables

  • Respond to inbound leads within 5 minutes during working hours, or flag the team if you can't.
  • Log every touch when it happens — not at end of day, not in a batch.
  • Every booked call gets a structured handoff note before the call starts, not after.
  • Every call outcome (show, no-show, close, lost) gets reported back to whoever booked it, same day.
  • Post your EOD report daily, in the right channel, every day — no exceptions for a slow day.
  • State your BAM and Reach out loud every Monday. A missed BAM gets a same-day conversation, not a quiet slide into next week.
  • When a doc and a live instruction conflict, the live instruction wins — and you flag the doc conflict so it gets fixed once.
  • Bad news travels same-day. A dead lead, a blown call, a missed target — say it now, not at the next scheduled check-in.
