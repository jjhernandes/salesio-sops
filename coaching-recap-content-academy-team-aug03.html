Context

What this session is

Who's on it
JJ's first sync with the full Content Academy sales team: Mino, Justin, Omar, and Allan, setting up the weekly cadence and coaching relationship going forward.
Opening context
The team just closed a $200K month, jumping from $165K to $200K in the final two days on a team-wide push, everyone closing, including Prosper (a coach) jumping on calls and Mino closing an upsell.
Core lesson
Selling on this offer is about awareness, not sales muscle. Know where you are in the process, and know where the prospect is on the awareness scale, problem-aware vs. solution-aware, so you never pitch too early or too late.
Walkthrough

What was covered

01
JJ's role: ancillary help, not a takeover
JJ frames the engagement clearly upfront: he's not here to run the team, he's here to make an already-working team better. He references his background (Iman Gadzhi, Alex Cattoni-adjacent names, Daniel Isles, Ashton Hall, and coaching top reps for teams like Dan Martell's) to establish credibility, then pivots straight into the actual point.
"A team with no goals is a team with no results... this is not me taking over anything, it's me coming in here and being able to be like an ancillary help to what you guys are already doing."
JJ Hernandez
02
The weekly cadence: Monday, Wednesday, Friday
Mondays, BAM & Reach. BAM is the bare-minimum cash-collected number each rep puts their name on for the week. Reach is the stretch number they're chasing on top of it, for themselves. Setting it live as a team creates external urgency and healthy competitiveness that wouldn't exist otherwise.
Wednesdays, midweek stat check. Where's everyone against their BAM, what do they need to close the gap, then get tactical, a call review or a roleplay depending on what's needed.
Fridays, culture day, with teeth. Wins and shoutouts, but also whichever of call review or roleplay didn't happen on Wednesday. If Wednesday was a roleplay, Friday is a call review, and vice versa, so the team is always sharpening something.
Tuesdays/Thursdays, send calls for review. JJ reviews calls sent to him on Discord and turns them around the same day.
Send your worst calls, not your best: "I don't need to be impressed by you guys... let's get you better by sending me your worst calls." Praise gets saved for Fridays, not handed out on request.
03
The awareness scale, and why "unaware" barely exists on this offer
Unaware → problem aware → solution aware → product aware → decision ready. JJ's point: because prospects come in through a full webinar they opted into and watched, true "unaware" is a near-impossible anomaly, under 5% of calls. Everyone booking a call already knows something, the job is figuring out what.
"There's two sides of awareness: your own awareness, and your awareness of the prospect... if you can understand both, then you guys are always in sync, and you never pitch too early or too late."
JJ Hernandez
04
Omar's question: does prospect sophistication change the approach?
Omar noted this ICP (20s-30s) feels more sophisticated than past offers he's sold (mostly 18-23), and that he doesn't need to make them "feel bad" about their situation, they already know they have a problem, the job is being solution-aware and selling the right program as the fix.
JJ's read: it's not really about age. It's about the prospect's perceived authority and how certain they sound, which can subconsciously shift a rep's frame (he used his own early career going from 18-year-olds to Myron Golden and Russell Brunson's team as the extreme version of this). The fix isn't a different sales approach, it's staying aware of when that shift is happening in your own head and not letting it change how you operate.
05
The call structure, kept simple on purpose
JJ was explicit this isn't a new process, it's the same one, taught with the "why" underneath it: Intro (respect first, likability later) → Current Reality → Problem Awareness → Solution Awareness → brief Future Pace/Consequence (only if the emotion isn't already there) → Pitch.
If they're already certain about their problem, don't keep interrogating them on it, move to solution awareness. If they're unsure, go deeper before moving on. The pitch itself should stay short.
"I call it a TikTok pitch. Under five minutes, pitch under five minutes on results only... they will assume in the positive with the less that they know."
JJ Hernandez
06
De-selling: the risk of over-qualifying a ready buyer
Because most prospects come off the webinar already fairly clear, extra unnecessary questions can create confusion instead of buy-in. JJ's own example: he closed people through DMs in 2023 for $2K deals because they were already that ready to go, no heavy process needed.
"Every decision is rooted in clarity... if you make them feel like they're more confused, then they're going to be like, well, I can't decide now."
JJ Hernandez
Allan's own admission fit this exactly: he's been going deep on current-state logistics rather than the actual problem, a habit he flagged on the spot as something to correct.
07
Identity-first coaching: belief, identity, action
JJ's closing point reframes what he's actually building with the team: not just a script, but who each rep is being while running it.
"Belief dictates identity, and identity dictates action... the vessel of who you are is so much more important than anything else I can teach you. If you're operating out of a place of shame, you're never going to perform."
JJ Hernandez
Not framed as "woo-woo," but as the actual foundation everything else, cadence, awareness, call structure, sits on top of.
