Structure

The 12-Step Sales Process — primary rubric for evaluating a call

Core Rule

Never pitch just to pitch. You only present once you've earned the right to present.

A strong close is created in discovery. If discovery is weak, objections get harder, the pitch feels forced, and the prospect won't feel clear enough to decide. Discovery is the close, everything after is confirmation.
01Intro: Respect First, Likability Later
Goal
Establish control, confirm time, open without overdoing rapport.
Script
"Hey [Name], good to meet you. I know you booked a call about [reason/context]. Do you still have the full [time] for today?"
Key principle — Don't try to impress them or become their best friend. Lead with calm authority.
02Current Reality
Goal
Understand what they're currently doing and where they are right now.
Questions
"Give me some context, what are you currently doing for [X]?"
"How long have you been doing that?"
"What's working? What's not working?"
Key principle — Start surface-level, then go deeper. Don't move on too fast.
03Problem Awareness
Goal
Help them become fully aware of the real problem and its impact.
Questions
"What do you feel is the biggest issue right now?"
"What have you tried to fix it, and why hasn't that worked?"
"What is this costing you right now?"
Redirect if they deflect
"I hear you, but if everything was exactly where you wanted it, why are we on this call?"
Key principle — Don't accept surface-level answers like "things are fine" if they booked a call. There's always a reason.
04Solution Awareness
Goal
Understand whether they know what the right solution actually looks like.
Questions
"How do you know what the right solution would even look like?"
"What would need to be true for you to feel confident moving forward?"
Key principle — This is often where they realize the gap is bigger than they thought.
05Future Pace
Goal
Create the gap between where they are now and where they want to be.
Questions
"If this was fully solved, what would be different?"
"What would life/business look like 3-6 months from now?"
Key principle — Don't get cheesy or overly emotional. Make it feel natural.
06Consequence / Cost of Inaction
Goal
Help them see what happens if nothing changes.
Questions
"What happens if this stays the same for the next 3-6 months?"
"Is staying where you are actually an option?"
Key principle — Only ask these if the emotion isn't already there. Overdoing it after strong discovery can feel manipulative.
07Transition to Pitch
Goal
Summarize their situation and earn permission to present.
Script
"Based on everything you told me, I do think this could be a really strong fit. The main thing I'm hearing is [problem], and that's creating [pain/consequence]. Let me walk you through exactly how we'd help."
Key principle — The pitch should feel like the obvious next step, not a random presentation.
08Pitch
Structure
Restate their current problem
Explain the mechanism that solves it
Show how the program works
Tie each feature back to their desired outcome
Check for understanding throughout
Key principle — Don't feature dump. Every part connects to something they already said. Under 5 minutes, no matter what's being sold.
09Confirm Fit
Script
"Based on everything we covered, do you feel like this is the right fit for what you're trying to solve?" — if yes: "Why do you feel that way?"
Key principle — Get them to verbalize the decision before price.
10Price Reveal
Script
"Cool. So the investment for the program is [$X]. Is that reachable for you?"
Key principle — Say the price calmly. Don't over-explain. Don't get weird about it.
11Objection Handling
If energy shifts
"I feel like the energy shifted a little bit. Am I wrong, or is there something I'm not seeing?"
If they say money
"Is it truly that the money is impossible, or is it more that you're not fully confident this will work for you yet?"
If they say "think about it"
"Of course. What specifically do you feel like you need to think through?"
Key principle — Don't argue. The objection stage should feel like home, calm, comfortable, curious.
12Close
Script
"Based on what you told me, this is the exact problem you said you wanted to solve. You also said staying where you are isn't really an option. So if the only thing left is getting started, let's take care of that now."
Key principle — They're committing to the new version of themselves, not just the program.
Philosophy

Core philosophy (the why behind the feedback)

"Sales is human." It's a conversation where someone has a problem, you might have something that solves it, and your job is to figure out honestly whether that's true, then help them make a clear decision.

"The best closers don't sound like closers." They sound like someone who actually cares whether this is right for the prospect.

"People buy based on emotions and compensate logically." Understand the feeling first, everything else comes after.

"We find out where you suck and make you good." Direct feedback is the culture, not an insult.

Discovery

Deeper discovery principles

Pick current-reality-first OR goals-first as the structure, not both, don't bounce between them mid-call.

When a real topic surfaces (revenue, a past bad experience), go deep before moving on. Always ask WHY behind a number: "500k a month, cool, why that number, what does that let you do?"

Address a bad past experience immediately when it comes up, or it resurfaces later as a much harder objection.

A loaded, identity-level statement ("I guess I'm just bad at this") gets a clarifying question, not an immediate deep dive: "What do you mean by that? How so?"

A rambling answer gets redirected, not chased: "You gave me a lot there, and I appreciate the context, but I want to make sure I understand where you're at right now. What would you say is the one biggest challenge?"

Pitch

Pitch principles beyond the script

"Sell the vacation, not the plane ticket" — sell the result and feeling, not the deliverables.

A deliverables dump creates confusion, not conviction. Only speak to what's already been established as important to this person.

Avoid the "mirror recap" habit (repeating back what the prospect just said). It's a dated guru tactic that reads as robotic. A genuine follow-up question does more.

Objections

Belief-identity-action layer

Change the belief and identity shifts on its own, guaranteed. Attacking identity directly doesn't guarantee anything changes, because the belief underneath never moved.

Anchor question: "What do you believe is not working?" The word "believe" specifically puts someone's mind in the right frame.

It's rarely an actual money objection, it's usually a value perception objection. If ROI isn't clear, staying put feels safer than the decision.

Presence

Frame, composure, and energy

Composure is the single strongest asset in sales. Reactivity is weakness (not the same as suppressing genuine emotion).

Sitting in silence while a prospect thinks is respect, not awkwardness. "50% of sales are lost in the first two minutes", if the prospect gains control early, it's hard to get back.

Stay more neutral early. Save warmth for once you're closing or real trust is built. Getting too friendly early removes the ability to challenge something later without it feeling jarring.

PACE buyer types: Practical (closed off, tactical), Action (high energy, go-go-go), Social (wins any room), Emotional (softer). Match tone to type, neutral energy eases anyone in regardless of type.

Reference

Glossary

Lead vs. Prospect
A lead hasn't talked to anyone yet. The moment they're on a call, they're a prospect.
Frame
Who's actually in control of the conversation and its energy. "Holding your frame" means staying grounded under pushback.
Cash Collected (CC)
Money that's actually landed today, distinct from total deal size on a payment plan.
Show Rate / Close Rate
% of booked calls where the prospect shows up / % of calls taken that turn into a sale.
Format

How to structure written feedback

01
Max two pieces of feedback per call. More than that is too much to actually implement on the next call.
02
Explain WHY before the fix. Say what it's costing them first, then the fix.
03
Rate specific moments numerically when useful (5/10, 6/10), direct and concrete, not harsh.
04
Reference the 12-step structure explicitly. "You skipped Problem Awareness, that's why the price objection landed so hard."
05
Be honest about what you can't verify. No transcript, no recording, no fabricated specifics, ask for one instead.
06
No filler, no over-apologizing. "Not bad, but could be better" is the right register, not "great job overall!"
