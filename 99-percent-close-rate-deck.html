The Real Mechanism

Five things. In this order.

Not five tips. A sequence — each one sets up the next.
01 Research
02 Memorable, not persuasive
03 Slower pace
04 The problem, stupid clear
05 Show the plan first
01 / Five

I obsess over research before we ever talk.

Before the call starts, I already know how they want to be communicated with, and how they actually buy.
01 / Five

You can't adapt to someone you're guessing at.

Research isn't prep work. It's removing the guesswork before it costs you the frame. By the time we're talking, I'm not finding out who they are, I already know.
02 / Five

I'm more memorable than I am persuasive.

Especially early. A unique opening frame — not the energy people expect the second they realize it's a sales call.

"Better to be memorable than persuasive."

Memorable creates a safety a typical rep never gives someone. That safety is what makes everything after it possible.
03 / Five

I move slower than almost every closer I know.

On purpose. To get the outcome I actually want, not just an outcome.
03 / Five

Speed doesn't build the relationship. Patience does.

Most reps are racing to the pitch. I'm not trying to get through the call, I'm trying to actually get somewhere with the person on it.
04 / Five

I get the problem stupid clear.

Not a vague complaint. Not the surface version they lead with.
04 / Five

The real problem, named so clearly they can't unsee it.

Ask why behind every number they give you. Address a bad past experience the moment it surfaces, or it comes back later as a harder objection. Depth here is what makes everything after it land.
05 / Five

I show them the plan before I ever ask for the sale.

A real call review. Their actual gaps, named. Exactly how we'd coach and develop them.
05 / Five

That's not a sales tactic. That's the value, delivered first.

Nothing's been bought yet. By the time there's an offer, they've already experienced what working with me actually feels like.
Recap

The order is the mechanism.
