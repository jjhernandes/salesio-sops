01

The Setter's Job

Three functions, in order of importance. Booking is the output, not the job.

1
Filter
Keep unqualified people off the closer's calendar. This is the job before it's anything else.
2
Prime
Create genuine curiosity and forward momentum before the call happens — the closer inherits whatever emotional state the setter leaves the prospect in.
3
Book
Get the right person on the calendar at a time they'll actually show. Not any person, at any time.
One bad set can poison two good ones. A bad set costs the closer 45 minutes, demoralizes them going into the next call, produces $0 revenue, and erodes closer trust in the whole pipeline. The prospect's first real interaction with Sales.io is the setter — a good experience makes them warm before the closer speaks; a "herded" one hands the closer friction they didn't create.
02

The 5-Phase Framework

This is the canonical structure — JJ's phase model, with Jalen's cold-call and DM mechanics built into Phases 1–2 as the channel-specific execution. Use this as the shape of every set, cold or warm, call or DM.

1
Pattern Interrupt
30–60 sec
Never open with what you do. Say their name once, calmly, then yours, then a two-beat pause — no "how are you," no company name up front. That combination is a universal sales-call tell prospects screen out in the first 3–4 seconds.
Cold Call"Hey Marcus." [pause] "This is Jalen."
DM / Warm"[Name] — not going to pitch you, just had a quick question about something you mentioned. You got 60 seconds?"
2
Hook + Quick Qualify
1–2 min
One curiosity line referencing their specific world, then one qualifying question in a curious tone, not an interrogating one.
Hook"I work with remote closers in the coaching space — specifically reps who've hit a plateau on close rate and can't figure out why."
Qualify"Quick question — are you on an offer you're happy with, or is performance the thing you're looking to fix right now?"
Disqualify, if it's a clean no"Honestly, based on what you've told me, I don't think this is the right fit for where you are right now."
3
Pain Extraction
3–5 min
The most important phase. Run this sequence in order, present-first — the current problem, then pace to the future. If you're talking more than 30% of this phase, you're doing it wrong; let them talk more than you.
Sequence"How long has it been this way?" → "What have you actually tried to fix it?" → "What has that cost you — and I don't just mean the money?" → "How much longer is this acceptable to you?" → "When this is actually solved, what does that look like for you?"
4
Bridge to the Call
1–2 min
Assume the yes and move straight to logistics. Never explain what the call covers in program terms — that's the closer's job, not yours.
Never Say
  • "On the call, we'll walk you through the program."
  • Anything that previews pricing, curriculum, or program structure — that's pitching, and it's not your job (see Qualifying Standard below for the one narrow exception).
Bridge"What does your week look like?"
5
Book & Lock
1 min
Give two specific time options, not an open-ended "when works for you." Send the calendar invite during the conversation, live, not after you hang up.
Book"I'd want to get you on a call with our head coach — not a pitch, just a real conversation. Tuesday afternoon or Thursday morning work for you?"
Reconciled: S10's cold-call framework (Interrupt→Hook→Qualify→Book) is the same shape compressed into a single call. This 5-phase version is the fuller model for anything with more room to breathe — DMs, warm inbound, community leads.

Channel Playbooks

Same framework, different mechanics per channel.

Cold Call — 4 Steps

StepWhatWhy
1. InterruptName, pause, your name. No greeting question.Prospects have a Pavlovian response to unknown numbers — the first 10 seconds decide engagement vs. tolerance.
2. HookOne specific line referencing their world.Generic openers get sorted into "sales call" instantly.
3. QualifyOne curious-toned qualifying question.Confirms it's worth continuing before you invest more time.
4. BookTwo binary time options.Open-ended scheduling questions kill momentum.

Tonality — 4 Principles

PrincipleIn Practice
Calm certainty over enthusiasmEnthusiasm signals you need their approval. "This works. I've watched it work." beats "This is SO exciting."
Slower than feels naturalPractice at roughly 80% of your normal speaking pace.
Downward inflection on statementsUpward inflection turns a statement into an uncertain question.
Consistent volumeNever go quieter or louder under pressure or after an objection — that's a tell.

DM Setting — Qualify, Don't Sell

"We don't sell in DMs. We qualify. The content already did the convincing — your job is to find the people it convinced." Never pitch in message 1. Never ask "want to jump on a call?" before pain, interest, and budget readiness have actually surfaced — that premature book attempt is the single most common DM mistake.

Opener TypeScript
New follower"What's going on [Name], just saw you shot me a follow. I speak a lot on [topic] — let me know if there's anything I can help with."
Commented on a post"Just saw you commented on one of my posts about [X]. Is this something you're currently going through?"
Liked content"Curious what's got you in that space right now — what are you working through?"
The filter: a real answer naming a problem or frustration means keep going. "Great content 🔥," "thanks man," or silence means move on — don't manufacture urgency that isn't there.
Sequence shape (condensed from the 10-message playbook): Expand the problem → get time-under-tension (how long has this been going on) → surface past investment history without asking a dollar figure → let them self-diagnose (don't drop social proof here) → qualify urgency → bridge to the closer with 2–3 time options → lock the time → get them to actually accept the calendar invite → set the expectation that the closer will reach out before the call.
The Bridge"Let's do this — I want to get you connected to my guy [Closer]. He's the person on our team who handles this. What times are you free later today, tomorrow, or the day after?"
"How much does it cost?""Price is tailored to what you need and I don't want to bullshit you — it's not a cheap investment. The exact number depends on your situation, and that's the call to have with [Closer]."
04

Qualifying Standard

Four criteria, all must be true before you book. Jalen's DM playbook calls this BANT (Budget / Authority / Need / Time) — same four things, DM-specific vocabulary, run Need first.

Budget Capacity
  • Ask readiness to invest, never a dollar figure directly — a direct number ("do you have $7,500?") creates defensiveness.
Script"Is investing in that kind of growth something you're in a position to do right now, or is timing off?"
Decision Authority
Script"For something like this — a real investment in yourself — is that a solo decision, or is there someone else you typically loop in?"
Timeline
Script"Is this something you're actively working on right now, or more of a down-the-road thing?" ("Now" = this quarter, not literally today.)
Genuine Interest
Distinguished from polite tolerance by whether they ask follow-up questions and take initiative, vs. one-word answers.

Minimum Bar to Book

Book ifDon't book if
Active income, not pre-revenuePre-revenue with no near-term income
Expressed a specific problem"Just curious," no real urgency
Open to investing in a solutionActive financial crisis
Decision is theirs, or partner is informedOnly polite tolerance — no genuine engagement
Partial qualifiers: interested + real problem but tight budget → book anyway, the budget conversation belongs to the closer. Interested + budget fine but an undisclosed spouse/partner → have them give the partner a heads-up before you book. Timeline is "next quarter" → don't book yet, follow up later. Standard: only book who you'd bet $375 on.

Disqualifiers & Required Action

SignalAction
"I just want to learn more first"Send to Skool community ($39/mo), tag for 60-day follow-up
Can't answer what offer they wantClarify further or disqualify
Refuses to discuss money at allAsk about financing; if still refused, not-yet
Already enrolled in a competing programLog it, check back in 3 months

Hostile or disrespectful in DMsDo not book, flag to JJ if severe
05

Objection Handling

"I'm not interested" is reflexive, not final — it usually means one of four things: they don't understand the offer, they've been burned before, they don't think it applies to them, or they don't trust you yet. You don't fight the objection, you get curious about it.

The VCR Pattern
  1. Validate — "That makes sense" / "Fair enough" / "Understood."
  2. Curiosity Question — genuine, not rhetorical.
  3. Reframe — specific to their actual answer, not a script recital.
ObjectionResponse
"I'm not interested""Fair enough — out of curiosity, is it that the timing's off, or that sales training specifically isn't something you're looking at right now?"
"I already have a coach/program""Got it — how's that going for you so far? What's it doing for your close rate?"
"I don't have the money right now""Understood — is the plan to revisit this once you're in a better place financially, or is the growth piece not a priority at all right now?"
"I'm not looking for anything right now""Makes sense — are you in a good spot with your close rate, or are you still working through things?"
"Send me more info" (brush-off)"I could do that, but honestly it won't give you what the conversation would. What would make a 20-minute call worth your time?"
Push When
  • Objection is reflexive, under 10 seconds
  • Engaged but hesitant
  • Stated objection isn't the real one
  • Revealed pain connects to the offer
  • Has qualified-prospect markers
Let Go When
  • Genuinely not a fit after 2 questions
  • Pre-revenue, no path
  • Financial distress
  • Said no twice with specific reasoning
Graceful exit"Understood — look, if anything changes on your end, I'll leave it open. Take care."
06

Booking & Confirmation

Industry average no-show rate on cold-outreach bookings is 40–60%. This sequence exists to beat that number. Three touchpoints, no more — a fourth crosses into chasing.

1
Immediate confirmation
within 5 min of booking
Must reference the specific pain point discussed — not a generic "looking forward to it."
Script"Just sent the link — looking forward to it. Based on what you mentioned about folding on the 'think about it' objection, I think you're going to get a lot of clarity from this call. You're confirmed for [day] at [time]."
2
24-hour check-in
day before
Re-anchor interest — this is the "mental vaccine" against canceling. Don't just confirm the time; restate the opportunity cost.
Script"Hey Marcus — tomorrow at 2pm, still good for you? One thing to think about before the call: what would your month look like if your close rate went from 18% to 28%? That's what we'll map out."
3
Day-of reminder
2 hrs before
Brief and human, with an easy reschedule path built in.
Script"Hey — you're on with Coach Jalen in about 2 hours at 2pm. [link]. If anything came up on your end, let me know and we can move it — otherwise see you there."
Leave Out
  • Corporate auto-emails
  • Generic "looking forward to it!" with no context
  • Re-pitching the program in a reminder
  • A 4th touchpoint before the call — three is the ceiling

Handoff Protocol

Starting a call in the dark costs the closer 5–10 minutes of rapport-building inside a 45-minute call. Every booked call gets a structured Close CRM note the moment it's confirmed — not the day of, not an hour before.

Handoff Note Template
NameMarcus Thompson
SourceCold call
SituationCurrently on $15K/mo in B2B SaaS. Wants to hit $30K.
Pain Point (their words)Has tried NEPQ, says it didn't stick.
QualificationBudget confirmed — looking at PIF or 2-way split.
Energy / ToneEngaged, asked follow-up questions.
Setter NotesBooked Thursday 3pm EST.
What doesn't count as a handoff: a name in Close with no notes, "he seemed interested," or a generic "qualified lead" tag with nothing behind it.

Daily CRM Discipline

"If it happened, it's in Close. If it's not in Close, it didn't happen."

RuleDetail
Log immediatelyEvery lead touched gets a note the moment the conversation happens — not end of day.
Opportunity created same dayEvery new lead gets a $7,500 opportunity created the day they're first contacted.
Stage disciplinePotential (new) → Call Booked (confirmed date/time) → Call Not Completed (3 attempts, no response in 48 hrs) → Nurture (not ready, needs a dated task) → Bad Fit / Not Interested.
7-day ruleNothing sits in "Potential" more than 7 days without a task attached.
Note formatWhat happened, what's the next step, who owns it and when — signed.
No duplicatesSearch before creating a new lead record.

Metrics & Diagnostics

Four levers, in funnel order. You're only ever broken in one or two of these at a time — find the biggest single percentage drop between two consecutive stages and fix that, not everything at once.

MetricTargetIf broken, fix
Connect Rate15–25%Dial volume, time of day, opener, platform. Below 5% = wrong audience/channel/time. Above 25% = possibly over-indexing on a warm list.
Booking Rate30–50%Pitch quality (treat the set like a mini-sale), who you're qualifying, urgency building. Below 20% = weak qualification or positioning; above 60% = you're probably not qualifying hard enough.
Show Rate70–85%Confirmation sequence, verbal commitment at booking, rapport built in the set. Below 70% is a real problem.
Show-Up Quality—Pre-booking questions creating a real filter; be explicit about who the call is and isn't for.
Don't fix the wrong metric. Worked example: 20% connect, 40% booking, 55% show — that's a show rate problem, not a connect or booking problem. Full math from a real coaching call: 120 DMs → 18 connects (15%) → 9 books (50% of connects) → 4 shows (44% of books). Diagnosis: the confirmation sequence was broken — only 2 of the 3 touchpoints were being used.

Volume Is Not the Goal

A setter who books 8 genuinely qualified calls a week is worth more than one who books 20 mixed-quality calls — every time. Volume for its own sake is ego. Track revenue generated per lead booked, not raw call count, and hold closer close-rate on your sets to the same bar as the team average.

The 6 Non-Negotiables
  • Every lead into Close CRM immediately — right when the conversation happens, not end of day.
  • Never promise a price or outcome you can't guarantee. "$7,500 all-in" is fine. "You'll definitely get placed in 30 days" is not.
  • No booking unless baseline qualification passes.
  • Respond to inbound leads within 5 minutes during working hours, or flag the team if you can't.
  • Do not pitch. Qualify.
  • Communicate bad news fast — cancels, no-shows, cold leads go into CRM + Slack immediately.

A Note on Sources

This doc pulls from the S10 setter curriculum, JJ's NCI setting framework, Jalen's DM playbook, Faris's daily standards, and the commission policy — several of which disagreed with each other. Here's what got reconciled and how:

Pitching: the older salesio-setting-script.html call script includes a "pitch — high-level overview" step that contradicts every other doc's no-pitch doctrine. Treated as legacy; the standing rule going forward is qualify only, per Non-Negotiable #5.
Price questions: S10 explicitly warns against asking for a dollar figure directly. The legacy call script does it anyway with a direct price anchor. Jalen's DM playbook never states a number at all. Canonical: indirect readiness-to-invest language, full stop — let the closer own the pricing conversation.
Show rate target: sources ranged from 70% to 85%. Set as a 70–85% band with 70% as the hard floor, matching the commission policy's "under 60% is a problem" language.
Booking time options: most sources use two ("Tuesday or Thursday"); Jalen's DM playbook uses three. Two is canonical for calls; DMs can flex to three given the async nature of the channel.
Discovery ordering: Fernando's framework (a different offer, Alex Eubank's brand) runs future-state before current-situation. Sales.io's core process runs current-problem first, future last. Both are documented here as offer-specific, not in conflict — use present-first as the Sales.io default.

Cross-Application: Social Era Media

A separate client engagement, not the Sales.io program — but JJ's own diagnostic principle showed up again word-for-word when consulting on it: a weak triage means a weak show rate downstream, regardless of closing skill. Same law, different business. Full SOP: social-era-setting-sop.html.

PrincipleTransfers Because
3 touches/day, hard capSame law as Sales.io's touch discipline — more isn't more, it reads as desperate either way.
Never double-dialSame instinct as never rushing the pattern interrupt — both read as "trying to sell me immediately."
Speed to lead: 1–5 minMatches Sales.io's <5 min inbound response standard almost exactly.
Respond the instant they re-engageIdentical rule, identical reasoning: that's the only window you actually have their attention.
Where it diverges: Chris Byrd's model leans harder into being visibly human — casual texting, memes as pattern interrupts, treating a booked call like showing up at a friend's house. Sales.io's model leans harder into authority and calm certainty. Worth testing pieces of the SEM warmth playbook against Sales.io's show-rate numbers before assuming they don't transfer.
