01

The Core Rule

When in doubt, communicate. The cost of over-sharing a status update is thirty seconds of someone's attention. The cost of under-sharing is a wasted call, a misquoted price, or a closer walking into a room blind.

Default To
  • Log the moment it happens, not at end of day
  • Flag a blocker the same hour you hit it
  • Say "I don't know, checking" instead of going quiet
  • Over-explain a handoff rather than under-explain it
Never
  • Sit on bad news hoping it resolves itself
  • Batch updates for a "big reveal" on Friday
  • Assume someone already knows because it seems obvious
  • Let a lead sit untouched without a note explaining why
02

Where Things Go: GHL vs. Telegram

Two systems, two jobs. Don't cross them.

SystemUse It For
GHLAnything specific to one lead — the setter brief, post-call notes, pipeline stage, SQL/NQL status. If it's about a person, it goes on her contact record, not in Telegram.
Telegram (team chat)Team-wide status: Friday KPI recaps, blockers, wins, help requests, anything the whole team needs to see.
Rule of thumb: if it's about one lead, it lives in GHL. If it's about the team's week, it lives in Telegram. A lead detail buried in a Telegram thread is a lead detail the next person won't find.
03

The Booking Sequence

Setter and closer both own pieces of this chain. Miss a step and the show rate takes the hit.

1
Setter books the call
Application reviewed, qualified against the 4 criteria, booked within 24 hours of application.
2
Closer sends the pre-call video, minutes after
30-45 second selfie video, sent the moment the setter's booking note lands. A stranger is easy to no-show — this is what makes the call feel real before it happens.
3
Setter sends the VSL homework link, right after
Goes out immediately following the closer's video, not hours later.
4
24-hour and 1-hour reminders
Both automated confirmation touchpoints before the call happens.
No-Show Protocol
  • 5-minute ping if she's not on the call
  • 15-minute mark: send reschedule link, move to No-Show stage
  • 24-hour follow-up
  • Two attempts with no response → move to Nurture
04

Setter → Closer: The Brief

Never skip the brief. Every booked call gets one in GHL before the closer joins.

Required in the Brief
  • Name, age
  • Her struggle, in her own words
  • Health issues or flags
  • Decision-maker status — is this her call alone, or does a husband need to be looped in
  • Whether the husband was mentioned
  • Whether she's watched the VSL homework, yes or no
  • Any red or green flags from the ICP screen
Before Every Call, Closer Checks
  • SQL vs. NQL status in LeadFi — especially for a lead that booked directly off the VSL with no setter screening. If she's NQL, either qualify her yourself on the call or cancel it. Don't run a full pitch on someone who was never actually screened.
A bad brief looks like: "she seems interested" with nothing behind it, a name in GHL with zero notes, or an SQL tag applied without checking if it's actually true.
05

Closer → Setter: The Loop Back

Communication runs both directions. A setter can't get better at qualifying if the outcome of her sets disappears into the closer's calendar and never comes back.

1
Every disqualified set gets flagged back to the setter who booked it
Not as a callout — as data. Why wasn't she qualified? That's the only way a setter's screening actually improves.
2
Outcome logged in GHL the same day: Closed Won, Closed Lost, or Decision Pending
A setter should never have to ask "did that call even happen?" three days later.
3
Only tag SQL when it's actually true
A lead that came in NQL but turns out qualified gets the SQL tag added on her GHL record — that's the whole action, automation handles the rest. Loose tagging corrupts the team's real ad-tracking numbers, so tag only what you can back up.
4
Objection patterns get said out loud, not just logged privately
If closers are all hearing the same objection this week, that's a setting-stage or offer-stage problem — it only surfaces if it gets said in Telegram, not buried in individual call notes.

Why Tracking Isn't Optional

Every number a setter or closer logs feeds a decision someone else makes with real money behind it.

1
It's the only way to find which lever is broken
A conversion problem looks identical from the outside ("numbers are down") whether it's a connect problem, a booking problem, or a show problem — unless the funnel is actually logged stage by stage in GHL.
2
It's what separates a setter problem from a closer problem
A closer flagging an unqualified set only means something if it traces back to who booked it. Without that, a bad week gets blamed on whoever's easiest to blame instead of whoever's actually responsible.
3
It's the difference between a $60K mistake and a caught one
The old funnel ran for a month burning spend with no return before anyone could prove it wasn't working. Tight tracking is what kills a bad bet in week one instead of month three.
4
It rolls up into whether the company hits $250K, then $500K
Every booked call, every SQL tag, every logged outcome feeds the same dashboard leadership uses to know if the funnel is actually working. Skip the log and that picture gets less accurate, not less real.
The honest version: a number that isn't logged isn't just missing — it's actively misleading, because everyone downstream assumes what they can see is the complete picture. Under-tracking is worse than no tracking, because it looks like data.

Non-Negotiables

  • Respond to leads within 5 minutes.
  • Post-call GHL note within 30 minutes — every call, no exceptions.
  • Never skip the setter brief before a closer joins a call.
  • Closer checks SQL/NQL status in LeadFi before every call, especially VSL auto-bookings.
  • Only apply an SQL tag when it's actually true.
  • No lead sits more than 5 days without logged activity.
  • Outcome of every booked call gets reported back to the setter who booked it, same day.
  • Post your Friday KPI recap in Telegram — calls, bookings, closes, cash collected, biggest win, biggest blocker.
  • When a doc and a live instruction from Khalil conflict, the live instruction wins — and you flag the conflict so it gets fixed once.
  • Bad news travels same-day. A dead lead, a blown call, a missed target — say it now, not at the next scheduled check-in.
