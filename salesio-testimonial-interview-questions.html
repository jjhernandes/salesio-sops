Sales.io — Manager Testimonial Interview Guide

The Interview That Proves They Stay

This interview is with the sales manager a rep landed under after our sourcing pipeline and 2-month training — not the rep themselves. A manager has no reason to inflate the story, which is exactly why their word proves the two things a prospective rep needs to believe: we get you into rooms you couldn't get into on your own, and we make you good enough that the person deciding who stays wants to keep you. These answers get clipped and sent through SendBlue as outreach to reps still on the fence.

How to Run This
  • Voice or video call with the manager, 15-20 minutes. Record it — you need the raw audio for clipping, not just notes.
  • Don't lead the witness. Ask the question, let silence sit, don't fill the gap with a suggested answer.
  • Push for a direct comparison once. A manager who's hired outside this pipeline before has a built-in point of reference — use it.
  • Get the rep's name and get the manager to say it. "This rep" is forgettable; "Marcus" is a testimonial.
  • Clip marks the questions most likely to produce a 10-20 second answer you can lift straight into an outreach text or short video.
01

The Hire

Why this rep made it past the point where most new hires don't.

Question 1
How long has [Rep] been on your team now?
Why: tenure from the manager's own mouth is a stronger proof point than a rep's self-report — it's the person who could've let them go, saying they didn't.
02

The Comparison

The single most valuable thing a manager can give you that a rep can't.

Question 2
How do they compare to other new reps you've brought on who didn't come through this kind of training? Clip
Why: this is the whole reason to interview the manager instead of the rep — a direct, credible comparison against everyone else they've hired.
03

The Proof

Numbers and specifics — what the manager actually sees on paper and on calls.

Question 3
What do their numbers look like — closes, revenue, whatever you track? Clip
Why: the single most important line in the whole interview. Get the exact number, not "they're doing well."
Question 4
What's something they do on calls or in their pipeline that most new reps don't? Clip
Why: concrete behavior beats a vibe — this is what makes "they're great" sound like an observation instead of a favor.
04

Staying Power

Most testimonials only prove someone got hired. This proves they're still wanted.

Question 5
Why do you think they've stuck around and performed, when a lot of new reps don't make it past the first few months? Clip
Why: this is the retention argument, from the one person whose opinion actually decides retention.
05

The Soundbite

Close with something quotable and short. Ask directly for it.

Question 6
If another manager asked whether they should take a rep from us, what would you tell them? Clip
Why: this is the line that goes straight into the outreach text — a manager vouching for the pipeline, not just the one rep.
Question 7
Would you take another rep from us?
Why: a direct yes, on record, closes the interview on the strongest possible note.
06

Using This in SendBlue Outreach

The interview is raw material. Here's how it turns into outbound texts to reps who haven't applied yet.

Pick one clip-tagged line per manager — the numbers, the comparison to other hires, or the direct recommendation. Don't stack quotes from multiple managers into one text; one credible manager beats three vague ones.
Sample outreach text"Hey [Name] — thought you'd want to see this. [Rep]'s sales manager at [Team] had this to say about reps we've placed there: '[clip-tagged quote].' Worth a conversation?"
Match the testimonial to the prospect. A rep eyeing a B2B role should hear from a B2B manager, not a B2C one. Keep a running library tagged by team type and offer, not just by outcome.
Built for the rep sourcing → 2-month training → placement pipeline. Run this with the manager a few weeks after placement, once there's real performance to point to — the best testimonials come from managers who've had time to actually notice.
