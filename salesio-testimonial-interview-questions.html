Sales.io — Testimonial Interview Guide

The Interview That Sells the Program

For reps who came through the sourcing pipeline and the 2-month training. The goal isn't a nice quote — it's proof of two specific things a prospective rep needs to believe before they'll apply: we get you into rooms you couldn't get into on your own, and we make you good enough to stay in them. These answers get clipped and sent through SendBlue as outreach to reps still on the fence.

How to Run This
  • Voice or video call, 15-20 minutes. Record it — you need the raw audio for clipping, not just notes.
  • Don't lead the witness. Ask the question, let silence sit, don't fill the gap with a suggested answer.
  • Push for specifics once. If they say "it was great," ask "what specifically changed" before moving on — one follow-up, not an interrogation.
  • Get their exact words on the before-state. That contrast is what makes the after-state land.
  • Clip marks the questions most likely to produce a 10-20 second answer you can lift straight into an outreach text or short video.
01

Before Sales.io

Get the real starting point in their own words. This is the contrast the rest of the interview depends on.

Question 1
Where were you at, sales-wise, right before you found us?
Why: gets the concrete before-state — income, role, how stuck they felt. Push for a number if they're vague.
Question 2
What almost stopped you from applying?
Why: surfaces the exact objection a hesitant prospect is feeling right now. Whatever they say here is close to word-for-word what you'll need to overcome in the outreach.
02

The Two Months

What actually happened in training — specific, not generic praise.

Question 3
What's one specific thing from training that changed how you sell? Clip
Why: "the coaching was great" is useless for marketing. "I stopped pitching and started asking why they hadn't fixed it yet" is a testimonial.
03

The Opportunity

This is the section that proves access — the thing a rep can't get by applying to job boards on their own.

Question 4
What team did you land on, and how would you have found that opportunity on your own? Clip
Why: the honest answer is usually "I wouldn't have" — that's the access story, and the direct comparison is what answers "why not just apply myself."
04

The Proof

Numbers. Specific ones, not ranges if you can get them.

Question 5
What are you closing or earning now, compared to before? Clip
Why: the single most important line in the whole interview. Get the exact number, not "way more."
05

Staying Power

This is the section most testimonial interviews skip, and it's the one that actually matters. Anyone can land a role. The real proof is that this rep is still good enough to keep it.

Question 6
How long have you been on this team now?
Why: tenure is the metric that separates "got placed" from "actually made it." Say it plainly, it's a strong number on its own.
Question 7
What do you see other reps struggle with that you don't anymore? Clip
Why: the retention argument in their own words — proof the training didn't just get them in the door, it's what keeps them performing after the excitement of a new role wears off.
06

The Soundbite

Close with something quotable and short. Ask directly for it.

Question 8
If a rep reading this is on the fence about applying, what would you tell them? Clip
Why: this is the line that goes straight into the outreach text. Let them speak directly to the exact person you're trying to reach.
07

Using This in SendBlue Outreach

The interview is raw material. Here's how it turns into outbound texts to reps who haven't applied yet.

Pick one clip-tagged line per rep — the income number, the "here's what I'd tell you" line, or the specific training-moment story. Don't stack multiple testimonials into one text; one sharp quote beats three vague ones.
Sample outreach text"Hey [Name] — thought you'd want to see this. [Rep Name] went from [before-state] to [specific result] after training with us and landing on [team/offer]. Here's what they said: '[clip-tagged quote].' Worth a conversation?"
Match the testimonial to the prospect. A rep stuck in a bad B2C offer needs a testimonial from someone who made that same jump — not a career-changer story. Keep a running library tagged by starting point, not just by outcome.
Built for the rep sourcing → 2-month training → placement pipeline. Update the question set as new cohorts graduate — the best testimonials come from whoever just lived through it most recently.
